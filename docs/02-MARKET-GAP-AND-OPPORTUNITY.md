# Opportunity decision

## Candidate concepts and scoring
Scores are 1–5, where 5 is favorable; complexity and maintenance score ease/affordability. Weights sum to 100. Scores are analyst judgments informed by document 01, not measured market sizes. Weighted score = sum(weight × score) / 100. No concept is established as “most profitable” by available data.

Harborlight: supply recovery and collaborative harbor repair. Collection and cooperative objectives have adjacent demand; art can begin with anchored primitives. Differentiation is visible restoration without taking others’ property. Retention scores only 3 because repair repetition is a real risk.

Wildlife kitchen: friends prepare food to restore habitats. Animal collection has current demand, but strong adjacent competition lowers saturation and differentiation scores. Recipes, animation and many animals increase maintenance.

Lantern railway: rebuild routes and deliver supplies to connected settlements. Shared travel and route planning could stand out; vehicles, physics and network ownership create the highest technical/maintenance risk. Demand is less directly evidenced.

Cooperative sky salvage: recover floating wreckage and rebuild airborne refuges. Exploration/co-op fit adjacent hits, but vehicles and traversal increase mobile and physics scope. Weather and layouts could support repeat play.

Neighborhood workshop: repair shops and decorate a shared street. Brookhaven and fashion show expression demand; compared with Harborlight, neighborhood building is less distinct and content production is heavier.

## Selected: Harborlight — Restore Together
USP: Every recovered supply visibly helps rebuild a storm-worn harbor with other players, while personal cosmetic progress stays yours. Working title, trademark availability unverified. Not a confirmed empty niche.

Player needs and feature fit: supply recovery gives a clear first action; shared projects make cooperation useful; contribution-based rewards give fair recognition; earned lantern colors support expression; recurring shifts give a natural stopping point; a separately labeled Christian reading garden serves voluntary exploration without economic incentives.

Commercial hypothesis: players who enjoy collection but dislike loss pressure may value calm cooperation and buy lasting cosmetic identity. Competition can copy mechanics quickly; art identity and reliable social experiences must carry the product. Cosmetics may monetize less effectively than paid power. The score lead is small; a two-point downgrade to differentiation can reverse the result. Do not authorize major production spending based on this matrix.

Validation: recruit 12–20 voluntary testers using platform-appropriate methods; observe comprehension, fun, independent return intent and actual returns. If cooperation is unused or gathering feels like work, change the loop before producing additional islands. No survey results currently exist. Research references and limitations: document 01.
